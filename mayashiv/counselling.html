01 Who it is for

  • Students · classes 9–12Stream and subject choices, board and curriculum decisions, study habits, exam pressure, and the confidence to state a preference out loud.
  • Students applying abroadApplication strategy, statement and interview preparation, and the cultural adaptation that begins long before the flight.
  • Undergraduates & early careersCourse and specialisation choices, first-job direction, interview readiness, and understanding what a role actually involves.
  • ParentsA calmer view of the options, what the child's signals mean, and how to support a decision without taking it over.
  • InstitutionsGroup sessions for a cohort or class — stream-selection workshops, exam-readiness sessions, study-abroad orientation.
  • Returning learnersAdults re-entering study or changing field, who need the options laid out plainly and without judgement.

02 What a session covers

An hour with the whole picture on the table.

Sessions are diagnostic first. Nothing is prescribed until the student's actual situation — marks, interests, constraints, family expectations — has been heard properly.

  • Academic roadmappingWhere the student is, what the realistic routes are, and what each route demands over the next two to four years.
  • Subject & stream choiceInterest and aptitude discussed honestly — including the cases where the obvious choice is the wrong one.
  • Study skills & pressureTime management, revision strategy, and separating useful nerves from the kind that costs marks.
  • Study abroad readinessApplications, interviews, budgeting, homesickness and cultural adaptation — drawn from years of AFS exchange work.
  • Career explorationMatching what the person is actually like to what the work actually involves, rather than to the family's favourite title.
  • Parent conversationsJoint sessions where the aim is a shared plan, not a verdict.

03 How it works

1 · Write

Email or call with the student's class or course, the current worry, and what a good outcome would look like. Two or three lines is enough.

2 · Schedule

A time is fixed by appointment — morning, evening or weekend — and a Zoom, Teams or Meet link is sent to you.

3 · Talk

A 45–60 minute one-to-one session. Followed by a short written summary of what was decided, on request.

  • FormatOnline, video or voice, one-to-one. Joint sessions with a parent are welcome and can be part of the same appointment.
  • Duration45–60 minutes for individuals; half-day or modular formats for school cohorts and workshops.
  • LanguagesEnglish · Hindi · Bengali · Tamil · Assamese
  • FeesShared before the appointment is confirmed. Nothing is payable until a time is agreed.

05 Confidentiality & boundaries

  • ConfidentialWhat is discussed in a session stays between the people in it. Nothing is shared with a school or a family member without the student's agreement.
  • NotesIf a summary is written, it is sent to the client and kept for continuity only — never circulated.
  • What this is notThis is educational and career counselling — not clinical therapy, diagnosis or medical treatment.
  • ReferralWhere a concern needs a psychologist, psychiatrist or medical professional, that will be said plainly in the session and a referral made. It is not something to be handled here.
  • MinorsSessions for anyone under 18 are arranged with a parent or guardian's knowledge and consent.
